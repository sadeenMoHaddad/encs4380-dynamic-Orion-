Control: PID toolkit (Homework 2)

Homework 1 showed how slow a system is. This section shows how feedback makes it faster and more accurate. Take the first-order plant from Module 1 with K = 1 and τ = 1 s: on its own it needs 3 s to reach 95%. With a proportional controller Kp = 9 the closed loop has τ = 0.1 s, ten times faster, but it settles 10% short. An integral term removes that error, and a derivative term calms the overshoot that follows.

How the simulation works

A closed-form answer does not exist for every gain setting, so every page steps the loop in time with RK4 (fourth-order Runge–Kutta), step h = 1 ms. The state is the plant output (and its derivative for a second-order plant) and the sensor reading. At each step the controller computes u from the error, u is clipped to its limit, and RK4 advances the plant with that u. The continuous PID updates every step; the digital PID of P3 updates every Ts and holds u in between. Source: control_lib.js.
