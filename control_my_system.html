My system: the Micromouse drive-motor speed loop

Our course project is a Micromouse. Each wheel is driven by a DC gear motor through a PWM motor driver, and an encoder measures the wheel. From PWM command (in %) to wheel speed (in mm/s) the motor behaves like a first-order plant, so P1–P3 apply directly. This page finds K and τ from a PWM step test and designs the speed loop.

1. The plant model and where its numbers come from

G(s) = v(s) / PWM(s) = K / (τ·s + 1) K in (mm/s) per % PWM, τ in s

The test: hold the mouse with its wheels off the ground, step the PWM from 0 to a fixed value, and log the encoder speed every 5 ms. K is the final speed divided by the PWM step; τ is the time to reach 63.2% of the final speed (Module 1).

2. The spec, and why it matters for the mouse

  

    
RequirementValueWhy

    
Damping ratio ζ0.6 ≤ ζ ≤ 0.8 (design for 0.7)
      The practical range from the lecture: fast, with only a few % overshoot. Too little damping makes the
      wheels surge and slip; too much makes every move slow.

    
Settling time (2%)≤  s
      The mouse speeds up and slows down for every cell (180 mm). The wheel speed must follow its profile
      within a fraction of a cell.

    
Steady-state error0Both wheels must hold the same speed or the mouse drifts sideways:
      a PI controller removes the error.

      the motor during a forward speed step.

PI on a first-order plant: closed loop τs² + (1 + K·Kp)s + K·Ki = 0

      ωn = √(K·Ki/τ),  ζ = (1 + K·Kp) / (2·√(τ·K·Ki))

      Design: ωn = 4/(ζ·ts),  Ki = ωn²·τ/K,  Kp = (2ζωn·τ − 1)/K
