P3: Digital PID

On an Arduino the controller does not run all the time. It reads the sensor once every sampling time Ts, computes u, and holds that u until the next sample. This page runs exactly that loop:

u[k] = Kp·e[k] + Ki·Ts·Σj=0..k e[j] + Kd·(e[k] − e[k−1]) / Ts

What to notice

  
    
    
  • Stair steps: u only changes at t = k·Ts (zero-order hold). Between samples the plant runs open loop.
    • 
    
  • Ts grows, performance falls: holding u for longer acts like a delay of about Ts/2. The overshoot
      grows, and past a point the loop oscillates. A common rule: Ts at most one tenth of the closed-loop time constant.
    • 
    
  • Anti-windup: while u is stuck at its limit, a plain integral keeps adding error. When the output finally
      arrives, that stored sum pushes it far past the setpoint. With anti-windup on, the sum stops growing while u is
      saturated (conditional integration), so the overshoot is much smaller. The dashed curve shows the other setting.
